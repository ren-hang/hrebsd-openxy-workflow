From measurement to orientation: where the errors enter

EBSD indexing turns band positions on a flat screen into a crystal orientation. That step is only as good as the geometry assumed between the beam spot, the sample and the screen, and the pattern centre (PC) is the number that ties them together. Schematic and first-order estimates for our Thermo Pathfinder setup (70° sample tilt, 20 kV); measured numbers come from the validation runs in NOTES.md.

2. Side view: why the pattern centre matters

The PC is the point on the screen closest to the beam spot on the sample. Moving the stage or changing the working distance moves it; a camera that is tilted (elevation) changes the angles of the whole screen. Only the combination matters for the bands.
x*, y*, z* are fractions of the pattern width (EDAX/TSL convention, used by OpenXY and PyEBSDIndex). Defaults are our Pt(2) values (Pathfinder X = 16.22, Y = 5.88, L = 20.35 mm, converted).

3. What a wrong pattern centre does to the bands

solid = bands where the true PC puts them. dashed = the same orientation as seen by an indexer that assumes the PC in section 2. The two sets of lines should coincide; any gap is an error the indexer turns into a wrong orientation (or, in HR-EBSD, an apparent strain).
fixed: 0.5105, 0.706, 1.0175

Rules of thumb (first order):
• x*, y* error δ ≈ a rigid rotation of about δ/z* radians (0.01 → ~0.6°). It looks like a real misorientation.
• z* error Δz/z ≈ a uniform magnification of all bands; it mostly acts like a dilatation (volumetric) and only weakly on the deviatoric part.
• Camera elevation error Δε rotates the whole frame about the screen's horizontal axis by roughly Δε.
• Deba's 11.04° correction is this last one: a pure rotation about specimen X, left-multiplied.
